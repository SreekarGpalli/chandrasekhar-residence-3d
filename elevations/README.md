# Elevation Studies

Exterior-only variants of the Chandrasekhar Residence (G+2, Anantapur).
**No interiors** — partitions, furniture, and dollhouse floors removed.

Nav is three active versions: **V44 (SF Pullback) · V43 (Continuous Rods) · V41 (Clover Jali)**.

| Code | Name | Folder | Key Facade Concept |
|------|------|--------|---------------------|
| **V44** | **SF Pullback** | `version-forty-four/` | East rod screen removed, plain MS railings; whole SF block and its roof slab cut back to the bedroom line (x 9830), bath between bedroom and lift, walkable slab over bath/lift joined to the roof terrace; tiled lean-to roofs hung off it (east arm falls east, north arm falls north), MS spiral service stair, no mumty |
| **V43** | **Continuous Rods** | `version-forty-three/` | Continuous unbroken vertical rods (~4.87 m) from ground ceiling beam soffit to second-floor balustrade + recessed floor slabs |
| **V41** | **Clover Jali** | `version-forty-one/` | Bespoke 4-leaf clover heart breeze block screen on East facade + South solid wall with 3 slit windows |

Each version has one viewer — the photoreal pipeline.

## Version Forty-Four

Built from V43. Exterior only. The whole east feature is gone — SE rod box, south slit-window wall and the FF splayed east box — and the FF/SF east decks now run to the full east lip (x 17362) with plain MS railings on the east and SE-south edges. On the second floor the east bedroom moves west into the old bath/walk-in strip (x 4920–9600), and the whole SF block — bedroom and family room — now ends at that bedroom wall (x 9830) instead of x 12650. The roof slab (and roof terrace, solar array, parapet) is cut back to the same line. The bedroom's bathroom (2590 × 1575) sits between the bedroom and the lift (x 9830–12420, y 230–1805), its north wall in line with the lift's north face. Everything east of x 9830 on the SF is open terrace. The bedroom gets a window (no door) and the family room its glass wall on the new east face. The SE pergola is removed. A flat walkable slab now covers the bath + lift and joins the main roof terrace as one terrace, with one parapet running round the outside. Two tiled lean-to RCC slabs with Mangalore clay tiles (15°) hang off that terrace from the top of its parapet (coping level, L.roof + 1.11 m), with no posts: one off the main slab's east edge (x 9830), falling east to the old east-face line (x 12650); one off the bath/lift slab's north edge (y 2035), falling north the same 2.82 m. They meet in a valley from the terrace's inner corner to the roof's inner corner. The stair mumty is removed and the roof closed over the stairwell; an MS spiral service stair (1.5 m dia, 100 mm centre column, 15 risers of ~224 mm, 20° chequer-plate wedge treads with nosings, two balusters per tread and a continuous helical handrail) stands in the open SF terrace just east of the lift, clear of both tiled roofs. It is entered from the north-west, turns clockwise, and its quarter-circle top landing opens straight onto the bath/lift terrace through a gap in the east parapet, with a guard on the landing's open edge. The terrace loungers moved north, out of its way. The east tile slope runs the full length of the east parapet, to the NE corner of the roof terrace.

## Version Twenty-Nine

Built from V27. The first pass at this was a detailing pass — shadow gaps,
a teak corner, a tidier roofline — and it did not work, because what made
V27 read as an ordinary G+2 was never the detailing. It was the massing:
three identical balconies of identical depth, stacked on three identical
columns, over a portico. Repeat one element three times and no amount of
trim makes it a contemporary house.

So the six moves below are mostly massing, and the trim follows.

| # | Move | What it replaces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No columns.** Two white blades, turned end-on to the street — one full height, one stopping at the second floor. The third is not replaced; the stair tower is behind it. | Three 300 mm "plus"-section columns with stone bases, steel storey collars and copings |
| 2 | **The three floors stop being the same floor.** Ground is an open car port between the blades; first floor is a glass tray; the second floor is CLOSED — its whole east face is a teak screen slab to slab, so the top storey is a screened volume on a white house. | Three open balconies of equal depth behind three different balustrades |
| 3 | **The corner portal goes to the ground.** V27's SE rod box began at the first-floor slab and stopped at the terrace, and was white rods in a white frame, so past ~15 m it vanished. It now runs 10.5 m in one piece and is lined in teak. | A white-on-white screen panel between two slabs |
| 4 | **A flying eave.** 780 mm of slab projecting past the entire east face, 200 mm thick, parapet set fully behind it. | A parapet flush with the wall below |
| 5 | **Skin and slots.** Each storey on N/S/W carries a plaster skin 90 mm off the structural face, stopping 45 mm short of each floor line — so the horizontals are a real 90 mm groove. The skin is cut with full-storey slots grouping the window clusters, backed in a grey plaster. Nineteen punched holes became seven apertures with no opening resized. | Sixteen applied charcoal-and-brass datum ribbons; per-window chajjas and sill ledges |
| 6 | **One balustrade everywhere.** Frameless glass in a base channel, on every FF/SF deck edge and both stair voids. | Plaster edge walls, bar railings, perforated steel panels and plaster pillars — four balustrades on one floor |

Plus: the service band's two mild-steel window grilles become three teak
panels, one per room, on the piers the internal walls already stand on; the
water tanks come down from 900 mm stands to 340 and get a batten enclosure,
so nothing mechanical is in the silhouette; the entry gets a freestanding
blade standing 800 mm clear of the facade and a bracketless canopy.

Materials, in full: white plaster, a grey plaster for slot backs, teak,
glass, dark steel. Teak appears in exactly three places and they read as one
system — up the SE corner for the full height, along the second floor east,
and across the south service band.

**Defects fixed in passing** (all present in V27): the fourteen north wall
lamps were drawn at `NB[0] − 70`, i.e. 70 mm *inside* the north wall, so
every one of them was in a bedroom; the lift shaft's north-wall flank ran
out to 14270 and duplicated the east wall over their shared 230 mm of y,
putting two faces on one plane (47 ray hits' worth of z-fight); the mumty's
two x-direction wall runs did the same at x 5715. `patternedRail`,
`patternedParapet`, `edgeWall`, `railPillar`, `railPillarsAlong` and
`lastBayStart` are deleted rather than left defined-and-uncalled — V19's
notes below record how much time the last set of those cost.

Verified: 134,756 triangles (V27 is 191,132), all 54 surface types
explicitly textured, console clean, and **zero coincident building
surfaces** on a four-face raycast sweep — V27 has nine such pairs.

### Real vegetation and real grass

Billboards are gone. A whole-tree photo on a quad has no thickness, shows its
own edge from the side, and disappears from overhead; crossing two of them
improves the silhouette and nothing else.

The four photos are now a **source of leaves**, not of trees. A patch is located
in each image by search — the greenest block for the trees and hedge, the
pinkest for the bougainvillea, because a hard-coded crop fraction gave the
shrubs a green card and they came out as dark blobs — cropped, given a ragged
alpha, and lifted in brightness (canopy photos are exposed for the sky, so their
leaves sit far darker than leaves under our own sun). Trees are then built like
trees: tapered trunk, three branches, and ~190 leaf clusters scattered through a
canopy *volume*, weighted outward so the interior stays open. The hedge is what
a clipped hedge actually is — a solid core with cards packed over its faces.

The lawn was one flat green box. It now carries a painted turf texture on the
slab (world-projected: the merged geometry's UVs are planar and smear on
anything horizontal) plus ~7000 crossed blade tufts.

All of it is instanced — 16,530 instances in 5 draw calls. Verified: 15,990
instance positions checked against the compound, 0 breaches.

The viewer was a black studio with fog at 45–95 m on a 230 m site, which greyed
out everything past the wall. Now daylight: horizon-haze background, fog pushed
to 150–520 m, and the site ground lifted off near-black (0x2a2824) to dry
Anantapur earth, which only looked right once there was a sky above it.

## Photoreal pipeline (V19 · V21 · V22)

The geometry lives in each version's `houseScene.js`. The photoreal work
lives in that version's `index.html` — SSGI, SSR, temporal accumulation,
filmic grade. V19 is the source; V21 and V22 start as copies.

### The pipeline

```
scene (HDR) ─┐
normals+refl ┤→ SSGI ──┐
             ├→ SSR  ──┼→ composite ─→ temporal accumulate ─→ lens + filmic → screen
             └─────────┘   (AO, bounce,   (jittered, 64 spp)     (DOF, CA,
                            reflections,                          ACES, grade,
                            edge cavity)                          vignette, grain)
```

Four things separate a viewer from a photograph, and this has all four:

1. **Indirect light.** Real shade is lit by light that bounced off something
   nearby and carries its colour. Screen-space GI, one bounce, cosine-sampled,
   2 rays/px. Stored as a *difference from a sky reference*, not as radiance —
   see the gotchas.
2. **Convergence.** A jittered projection (Halton 2,3) accumulated over 64
   samples. Holding still resolves the image: edges, GI and reflections all
   clean up together. Converges in ~220 ms.
3. **Reflections.** Screen-space, Fresnel-weighted, with a sky-gradient
   fallback for rays that leave the frame, so windows carry the real building
   and the real car.
4. **Imperfection.** World-space grime where water runs and splashes back;
   leaf cards scattered over the canopy shells; a screen-space cavity term that
   gives every arris the highlight a real chamfer catches; and the downpipes,
   AC condensers, dish, meter box and service drop that every real house has.

Materials: procedural PBR kits (albedo + normal + roughness) for plaster,
stone, concrete, teak, tile, paver, asphalt, grass, earth, farmland, leaf,
bark, metal and car paint, at 2K on the high tier, re-projected to world-space
UVs per surface. Reflectance is compressed into a physical range
(`0.035 + 0.76·c`) so nothing is brighter than real lime or darker than real
bounce.

Three sun presets (morning / midday / golden) re-light, re-sky, rebuild the
IBL and re-bake the shadow map. Six camera presets. `window.__el` exposes
`{scene, camera, renderer, data, applySun, applyView, look, resolve, render}`
plus `POST.dbg` (every pass and render target) for geometry audits and
offscreen capture — call `resolve()` before reading the canvas, or you capture
a cleared buffer.

### Gotchas, all of which cost real time here

- **π irradiance.** three r128 multiplies direct irradiance by π when
  `physicallyCorrectLights` is off, so a directional intensity of 3+ on a
  0.87-albedo wall clips flat at the top of the tone curve and *hides the
  shadows entirely*. It looks exactly like a broken shadow map. It is not.
- **A raw ShaderMaterial is not colour-managed.** The sky dome skips three's
  tone mapping *and* its output encoding, so writing sRGB-valued numbers there
  looks correct on a direct-to-canvas render purely by accident — the canvas
  reinterprets them. Feed the same numbers to an HDR buffer that a real tone
  map reads as radiance and the sky blows to white haze. Sky, fog and clear
  colours must be converted to linear when the pipeline is on.
- **Sky is not a depth threshold.** The dome sits at r=1000 against a far plane
  of 1200, so its window depth is 0.99991 — under any "close enough to 1.0"
  epsilon you would pick. Identify sky from the all-zero normal buffer instead.
- **Every visible mesh needs a normal-buffer material.** One left out doesn't
  go missing from the buffer; it renders with its ordinary material, so its
  albedo lands in the normal channels and its opaque alpha reads as "fully
  reflective". That is how the distant fields became a lake.
- **Raw SSGI floods a forward renderer.** The forward pass already lit
  everything with an IBL assuming open sky, so adding bounce radiance on top
  double-counts. Store the ray result as (hit − sky) and it self-corrects.
- **Depth of field must be a thin-lens model.** Defocus goes with the
  difference of *reciprocal* distances. A linear `|z − focus|` saturates
  everything past the subject, so the sky lands on maximum blur and the clouds
  smear into a flat wash.
- **alphaTest cut-outs die in the mip chain.** A leaf mask painted at per-leaf
  scale averages to ~0.8 alpha two mips down and the test passes everywhere —
  a cut-out canopy silently becomes a solid balloon. Paint the mask at clump
  scale.
- `pmrem.fromScene()` over the custom sky ShaderMaterial yields a malformed env
  map that NaNs every PBR surface. `fromEquirectangular` is the only safe path.
